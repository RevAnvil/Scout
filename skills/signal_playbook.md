# RevAnvil Signal-Based Outreach Playbook

Scout's job is to find a credible reason RevAnvil should contact an industrial company now. Signals are evidence, not decoration. Never fabricate a software stack, hiring plan, customer issue, revenue figure, or operational problem.

## Core rule

Use exactly one strong signal as the reason for outreach. Do not stack several facts into a creepy dossier. If no verified signal exists, fall back to a true industrial revenue-leak problem that RevAnvil commonly sees.

## Signal priority for RevAnvil

Rank signals roughly in this order:
1. Relevant hiring signal
2. ERP/CRM or sales-operations change
3. New sales leadership / commercial expansion
4. RFQ-heavy or quote-driven sales motion
5. Multi-location / complex recurring-account structure
6. Public evidence of manual process friction
7. Generic ICP fit only

## Hiring signals

Relevant roles:
- Revenue Operations
- Sales Operations
- CRM Administrator
- ERP Administrator
- Inside Sales
- Sales Manager
- Business Development

Angle: the workload behind the hire, not "congrats on hiring."

Good:
"Saw you're hiring for sales ops. Usually means there's enough activity that somebody needs to own the process between the ERP, CRM, and reps."

Bad:
"Congrats on your exciting growth!"

## ERP / CRM signal

Only mention software if it is actually verified. Never assume a company uses P21, Epicor, Infor, RubberTree, HubSpot, Salesforce, or any other system.

If a system is verified, the angle is not "we integrate with your software." The angle is whether the current system surfaces problems early enough for the sales team to act.

Good:
"If P21 is where most of the customer history lives, does anyone get flagged when an account starts quietly buying less?"

Bad:
"We noticed you're on P21 and can optimize your ERP."

When software is unknown, use language like:
"If you're running something like P21, Epicor, Infor, or another ERP/CRM..."

## RFQ / quote-driven motion

Evidence can include RFQ forms, quote-request pages, large configurable catalogs, engineering/contact-sales workflows, or clear inside-sales activity.

Angle:
- missed quote follow-up
- quotes aging without ownership
- lack of visibility into quote-to-order conversion

Good:
"Looks like a lot of your sales motion runs through RFQs. How are older quotes getting surfaced before they disappear?"

## Recurring-account / catalog complexity

Useful indicators:
- broad industrial catalog
- repeat-replacement parts
- branches/territories
- distributor/dealer structure
- service/repair component
- recurring B2B accounts

Angle: account decline becomes invisible until the revenue gap is already obvious.

Good:
"With that many product lines and repeat accounts, I'm curious how your team catches a customer whose purchasing is slowly falling off."

## Expansion / new leadership

Signals:
- new branch/location
- acquisition
- new VP Sales / GM / President
- territory expansion
- substantial commercial hiring

Angle: visibility gets harder as responsibility spreads across people, territories, and systems.

Do not imply revenue problems simply because a company is expanding.

## Proof points RevAnvil may use

Approved proof:
- One distributor had 68 customers disappear year over year, representing about $680k in prior revenue.
- RevAnvil has also found missed quote follow-up and accounts declining for months.

Never present the $680k as guaranteed recoverable revenue. It is prior revenue associated with dormant customers, not a promise of recovery.

## Email style

RevAnvil emails should:
- make sense within five seconds
- be short
- sound like a person, not an agency
- avoid "I hope this finds you well," "following up," "circling back," and generic AI/automation hype
- use real proof when useful
- end with one simple question
- avoid links in cold first-touch emails unless specifically approved

## Current campaign worldview

RevAnvil's strongest current positioning is:

**Industrial companies are already buried in pitches for AI, leads, software, and automation. Most do not need another platform. They need to get more from the customers and systems they already have.**

The subject / running joke currently in use is `enough is enough`.

Do not force that exact copy into every campaign. Preserve the underlying idea: RevAnvil is not another shiny software pitch.

## Follow-up logic

A follow-up should add a new dimension rather than restating the first email.

Useful second-touch angles:
- dormant customers were only one leak
- missed quote follow-up
- declining accounts
- existing ERP/CRM data not surfacing risk

Never make RevAnvil sound like it only performs CRM/ERP analysis. Software visibility is one path into a broader commercial revenue-leak diagnostic.

## No-signal fallback

When there is no verified current signal, use a true industry problem rather than fake personalization.

Examples:
- "Most distributors don't realize an account is declining until the revenue gap is already obvious."
- "Quote follow-up tends to become invisible once volume gets spread across several reps."
- "A lot of useful customer history already sits in the ERP; the issue is whether anybody gets a clean flag when behavior changes."

The goal is relevance without pretending we know something we do not.
